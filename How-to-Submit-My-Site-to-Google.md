**What Does “Submit My Site to Google” Mean?**
----------------------------------------------

Submitting your site to Google means helping Google discover your website pages so they can be crawled and considered for indexing.

Google Search Console allows website owners to submit sitemaps and individual URLs for crawling, review index coverage, and understand how Google Search sees their pages.

In simple words:

**Submitting your site to Google helps Google find your website pages faster.**

However, submission is not the same as ranking. A submitted page still needs to be crawlable, indexable, useful, and relevant before it can perform well in search results.

**Step 3: Set Up Google Search Console**
----------------------------------------

The best way to submit your site to Google is by using **Google Search Console**.

Google Search Console is a free tool that helps website owners monitor search performance, submit sitemaps, inspect URLs, and review indexing issues. Google describes Search Console as a tool that helps you measure search traffic, fix issues, submit content, and understand how Google Search sees your pages.

To set it up:

1.  Go to Google Search Console.
    
2.  Sign in with your Google account.
    
3.  Click **Add property**.
    
4.  Enter your website.
    
5.  Choose a property type.
    
6.  Verify ownership.
    

You will usually see two property options:

**Property Type**

**Best For**

**Domain Property**

Tracks all versions of your domain, including http, https, www, and non-www

**URL Prefix Property**

Tracks only the exact URL version you enter

For most website owners, a **Domain Property** is the better option because it covers the full domain.

**Step 4: Verify Your Website Ownership**
-----------------------------------------

Google needs to confirm that you own the website before showing private search and indexing data.

Common verification methods include:

*   DNS TXT record
    
*   HTML file upload
    
*   HTML meta tag
    
*   Google Analytics
    
*   Google Tag Manager
    

For a domain property, DNS verification is commonly used. Google gives you a TXT record, and you add it to your domain DNS settings.

Once verification is complete, you can submit your sitemap and inspect URLs inside Google Search Console.

**Step 5: Create an XML Sitemap**
---------------------------------

An XML sitemap is a file that lists important URLs on your website.

A sitemap helps Google discover your pages more efficiently. Google’s sitemap documentation explains that a sitemap can help Google learn which pages and files are important on your site, but submitting a sitemap is a hint and does not guarantee crawling or indexing.

A sitemap usually looks like this:

https://example.com/sitemap.xml

Your sitemap should include:

*   Homepage
    
*   Blog posts
    
*   Service pages
    
*   Product pages
    
*   Category pages
    
*   Landing pages
    
*   Important resource pages
    

Your sitemap should not include:

*   Admin pages
    
*   Login pages
    
*   Thank-you pages
    
*   Broken URLs
    
*   Redirected URLs
    
*   Duplicate pages
    
*   Noindex pages
    
*   Thin or low-value pages
    

A clean sitemap gives Google a better list of the pages you want discovered.

**Step 6: Submit Your Sitemap to Google**
-----------------------------------------

After creating your sitemap, submit it in Google Search Console.

Follow these steps:

1.  Open Google Search Console.
    
2.  Select your website property.
    
3.  Click **Sitemaps** from the menu.
    
4.  Enter your sitemap URL.
    
5.  Click **Submit**.
    

Example:

sitemap.xml

or:

https://example.com/sitemap.xml

Google’s sitemap documentation confirms that sitemaps can be submitted through the Search Console Sitemaps report, where you can also see processing errors.

**Step 7: Submit Important URLs Manually**
------------------------------------------

**Step 8: Make Sure Google Can Crawl Your Site**
------------------------------------------------

Submitting your site is useful only if Google can crawl it.

Check these common crawl-blocking issues.

### **Robots.txt Blocking**

Your robots.txt file tells search engines which pages they can or cannot crawl.

This rule can block the whole website:

User-agent: \*

Disallow: /

If this is active, Google may not crawl your site properly.

Make sure important pages are not blocked by robots.txt.

### **Noindex Tag**

A noindex tag tells Google not to index a page.

Example:

If you want a page indexed, remove the noindex tag.

### **Wrong Canonical Tag**

A canonical tag tells Google which version of a page is preferred.

If your canonical tag points to another URL, Google may choose not to index the current page.

Make sure canonical tags point to the correct URLs.

**Common Mistakes When Submitting a Site to Google**
----------------------------------------------------

### **1\. Thinking Submission Guarantees Ranking**

Submitting your site helps Google discover it, but it does not guarantee top rankings.

Ranking still depends on content quality, backlinks, user experience, technical SEO, search intent, and competition.

### **2\. Submitting Low-Quality Pages**

Not every page should be indexed.

Avoid submitting:

*   Duplicate pages
    
*   Thin pages
    
*   Admin pages
    
*   Thank-you pages
    
*   Internal search pages
    
*   Empty category pages
    
*   Low-value tag pages
    

Focus on useful pages that help users.

### **3\. Blocking Google by Mistake**

Many indexing problems happen because Google is blocked by robots.txt or noindex tags.

Always check crawl settings before submitting your site.

### **4\. Ignoring Google Search Console**

Google Search Console gives important indexing data. If you do not check it, you may miss sitemap errors, crawl problems, and excluded pages.

### **5\. Forgetting Internal Links**

Pages without internal links can be harder for Google to discover.

Every important page should be linked from at least one relevant page on your website.

**FAQs About Submit My Site to Google**
---------------------------------------

### **How do I submit my site to Google?**

You can submit your site to Google by setting up Google Search Console, verifying your website, submitting your XML sitemap, and requesting indexing for important URLs.

### **Is it free to submit my site to Google?**

Yes. Google Search Console is free to use.

### **How long does it take Google to index my site?**

There is no fixed time. Some websites or pages may be indexed quickly, while others may take days or longer. Indexing depends on content quality, crawlability, internal links, technical SEO, and Google’s evaluation of the page.

### **Does submitting my site guarantee indexing?**

No. Submitting your site helps Google discover your pages, but Google decides whether to index them. Google also states that submitting a sitemap is a hint, not a guarantee.

### **How do I check if my site is indexed?**

Search Google using:

site:yourdomain.com

You can also use Google Search Console’s URL Inspection Tool for more accurate page-level indexing information.

### **Why is my site not showing on Google?**

Your site may not show on Google because it is new, blocked by robots.txt, marked with noindex, affected by duplicate content, has thin pages, lacks internal links, or has technical errors.
