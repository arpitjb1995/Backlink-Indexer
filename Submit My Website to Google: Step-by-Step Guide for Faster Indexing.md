**What Does “Submit My Website to Google” Mean?**
-------------------------------------------------

Submitting your website to Google means helping Google discover your website pages so they can be crawled and considered for indexing.

Google uses crawlers to find web pages. Once Google finds a page, it analyzes the content, checks links, understands the page, and decides whether to add it to the Google index.

In simple words:

**Submitting your website to Google helps Google find your site and consider your pages for search results.**

However, submission is only the first step. Your website also needs helpful content, clean technical SEO, internal links, and crawlable pages.

**How to Submit My Website to Google Step by Step**

**Step 1: Make Sure Your Website Is Ready**
-------------------------------------------

Before submitting your website to Google, make sure your site is live and accessible.

Check that:

*   Your domain opens correctly

*   Your website is not password-protected

If your website is not accessible, Google may not be able to crawl or index it.

**Step 2: Create Helpful Website Pages**
----------------------------------------

Google is more likely to index pages that provide value.

Before submitting your website, make sure your main pages are useful and complete.

Important pages may include:

*   Contact page
    
*   Service pages
    
*   Product pages
    
*   Blog posts
    
*   Category pages
    
*   Location pages
    

Each page should have a clear purpose, original content, proper headings, and useful information for visitors.

Thin, duplicate, or empty pages may have a lower chance of being indexed.

**Step 3: Set Up Google Search Console**
----------------------------------------

The best way to submit your website to Google is through **Google Search Console**.

Google Search Console is a free tool that helps you check how Google sees your website. It allows you to submit sitemaps, inspect URLs, monitor indexing issues, and track search performance.

To get started:

1.  Open Google Search Console.
    
2.  Sign in with your Google account.
    
3.  Click **Add Property**.
    
4.  Enter your website domain.
    
5.  Choose a property type.
    
6.  Verify website ownership.
    

You will usually see two options:

**Property Type**

**Best For**

**Domain Property**

Covers all versions of your domain, including http, https, www, and non-www

**URL Prefix Property**

Covers only the exact URL version you enter

For most website owners, **Domain Property** is the better option because it tracks the full domain.

**Step 4: Verify Your Website Ownership**
-----------------------------------------

Google needs to confirm that you own the website before giving you access to Search Console data.

Common verification methods include:

*   DNS TXT record
    
*   HTML file upload
    
*   HTML meta tag
    
*   Google Analytics
    
*   Google Tag Manager
    

For a domain property, DNS verification is commonly used. Google gives you a TXT record, and you add it to your domain DNS settings.

After verification, you can start submitting your website to Google.

**Step 5: Create an XML Sitemap**
---------------------------------

An XML sitemap is a file that lists the important URLs on your website.

A sitemap helps Google discover your pages more easily.

A sitemap usually looks like this:

https://example.com/sitemap.xml

Your sitemap should include important pages such as:

*   Homepage
    
*   Blog posts
    
*   Service pages
    
*   Product pages
    
*   Category pages
    
*   Landing pages
    

Your sitemap should not include:

*   Admin pages
    
*   Login pages
    
*   Thank-you pages
    
*   Duplicate pages
    
*   Broken URLs
    
*   Redirected URLs
    
*   Noindex pages
    
*   Low-value pages
    

A clean sitemap helps Google understand which pages you want discovered.

**Step 6: Submit Your Sitemap to Google**
-----------------------------------------

Once your sitemap is ready, submit it in Google Search Console.

Follow these steps:

1.  Open Google Search Console.
    
2.  Select your website property.
    
3.  Click **Sitemaps** from the menu.
    
4.  Enter your sitemap URL.
    
5.  Click **Submit**.
    

Example:

sitemap.xml

or:

https://example.com/sitemap.xml

Submitting your sitemap helps Google find your website pages. However, sitemap submission does not guarantee indexing. Google still decides whether each page should be added to its index.

**Step 7: Submit Important URLs Manually**
------------------------------------------

After submitting your sitemap, you can manually submit important URLs using the **URL Inspection Tool** in Google Search Console.

This is useful for:

*   New blog posts
    
*   New service pages
    
*   Updated pages
    
*   Product pages
    
*   Landing pages
    
*   Pages fixed after indexing problems
    

### **How to request indexing**

1.  Open Google Search Console.
    
2.  Paste your full URL into the top inspection bar.
    
3.  Press **Enter**.
    
4.  Wait for Google to inspect the page.
    
5.  Click **Request Indexing**.
    

Example:

https://example.com/new-page

**Step 8: Make Sure Google Can Crawl Your Website**
---------------------------------------------------

Submitting your website will not help if Google cannot crawl your pages.

Check these common crawl-blocking issues.

### **Robots.txt Blocking**

Your robots.txt file tells search engines which pages they can or cannot crawl.

This rule can block your entire website:

User-agent: \*

Disallow: /

If your site has this rule, Google may not crawl your pages.

Make sure important pages are not blocked by robots.txt.

### **Noindex Tag**

A noindex tag tells Google not to index a page.

Example:

If you want a page indexed, remove the noindex tag.

### **Wrong Canonical Tag**

A canonical tag tells Google which version of a page is the main version.

If your canonical tag points to another URL, Google may choose not to index the current page.

Make sure canonical tags point to the correct URLs.

**Common Mistakes When Submitting a Website to Google**
=======================================================

**1\. Thinking Submission Guarantees Ranking**
----------------------------------------------

Submitting your website helps Google discover it, but it does not guarantee first-page rankings.

Ranking depends on content quality, backlinks, authority, user experience, technical SEO, and competition.

**2\. Submitting Low-Quality Pages**
------------------------------------

Not every page should be indexed.

Avoid submitting:

*   Duplicate pages
    
*   Thin pages
    
*   Admin pages
    
*   Thank-you pages
    
*   Internal search pages
    
*   Empty category pages
    
*   Low-value tag pages
    

Focus on useful pages that help users.

**3\. Blocking Google by Mistake**
----------------------------------

Many indexing problems happen because Google is blocked by robots.txt or noindex tags.

Always check crawl settings before submitting your site.

**4\. Ignoring Google Search Console**
--------------------------------------

Google Search Console gives you important indexing data. If you do not check it, you may miss crawl errors, sitemap issues, and excluded pages.

**5\. Not Adding Internal Links**
---------------------------------

Pages without internal links can be harder for Google to discover.

Every important page should be linked from at least one relevant page on your website.
